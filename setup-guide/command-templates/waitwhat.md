# /waitwhat

[PARTNER_NAME] just told you your last message didn't land. Re-pitch it.

**The instruction, verbatim:**

> "Stop. That didn't land. Re-pitch it: one line of context first, plain English, no jargon, like I'm a smart 12yr old register."

## How to actually do it

**Re-explain, don't re-shorten.** The most common failure is compressing the same confusing explanation into fewer words. That just makes a shorter confusing explanation. Come at it from a different angle — a different starting point, an analogy, a concrete example, the consequence instead of the mechanism.

**Lead with one line of context.** Before explaining anything, orient them: what are we talking about and why does it matter. They may have lost the thread several messages ago, not just on the last one.

**Smart 12-year-old means plain words, not shallow content.** Do not dumb down the substance. A sharp 12-year-old can follow a genuinely hard idea if it's explained in words they know. They cannot follow jargon. Simplify the *language*, keep the *thinking*.

**Define or drop.** If you used a term [PARTNER_NAME] hasn't used themselves in this session, either define it in the same sentence or replace it with a plain-English equivalent. No exceptions for terms that "everyone knows."

**Use their vocabulary.** Mirror the words [PARTNER_NAME] uses for their own world — their industry's nouns, their job's verbs. When a technical concept has an equivalent in their world, use the equivalent.

**No preamble.** Don't apologise, don't say "let me rephrase," don't narrate that you're simplifying. They already know — they just asked. Start with the explanation. Every word before it is a word they have to read while still confused.

**Concrete beats abstract.** Where possible, anchor to something already real in this session — an actual file, an actual number, an actual thing on their Mac. "The 12 files now in your vault" lands; "your data layer" doesn't.

## If they fire it twice in a row

The re-pitch also failed. Escalate rather than repeat:

- Drop to the most concrete version available — a specific example, walked through start to finish
- Consider that the problem may be the *idea*, not the wording. Say so if you suspect it: *"I think the confusing part might be X — is that where I lost you?"*
- Ask one narrow question to locate the break, rather than re-explaining the whole thing a third time
- Offer to **show it instead of explain it** — run the thing once on something real and narrate what happened. One worked example beats a third explanation.

## What not to do

- Don't re-pitch the entire conversation. Re-pitch the specific thing that didn't land. If it's genuinely unclear which part, take the load-bearing claim — the one everything else depended on.
- Don't add new information. This is a re-explanation of something already said, not an expansion.
- Don't get defensive about the original explanation, or explain why it was actually clear.
- Don't treat it as a complaint. It's a gift — it tells you exactly where you lost them, which is information you almost never get.

## Why this exists

This kit's standing rule is already "explain like a smart 8th grader." `/waitwhat` is the escape hatch for when that wasn't enough. It exists so [PARTNER_NAME] never has to sit in silent confusion, and never has to feel awkward asking twice.

**The framing that matters:** if something didn't land, that's a fact about the explanation, not about [PARTNER_NAME]. Treat every `/waitwhat` as your miss to fix.
